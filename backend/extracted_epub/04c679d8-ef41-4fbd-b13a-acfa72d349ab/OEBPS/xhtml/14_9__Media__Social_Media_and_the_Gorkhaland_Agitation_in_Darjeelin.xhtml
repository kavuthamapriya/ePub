CHAPTER 9

Media, Social Media, and the
Gorkhaland Agitation in Darjeeling

BINU SUNDAS AND DIVYA PRADHAN

INTRODUCTION

The Gorkhaland movement has a long history. It started with the Hillmen’s Association submitting a memorandum for a ‘separate administrative set-up’ for the district of Darjeeling to the then Governor General of Bengal in 1907. Subsequently, many memoranda have been submitted for separating the Gorkha-dominated areas from the province of Bengal. Since 1907, the creation of Gorkhaland has been entrenched in the collective conscience of the people. Another memorandum, a refined version of the 1907 memorandum was again submitted to the authorities in 1917, the representative of the Darjeeling district to the Chief Secretary, Government of Bengal, demanding the ‘creation of a separate unit’. The unit in the memorandum was defined as the territory of the Darjeeling district and the Doars areas of Jalpaiguri district (Subba: 1992). Soon the declaration by the Indian National Congress in its 1920 Nagpur Session, that in post-independence India, states would be formed based on linguistic identity further fueled the imagination of Gorkhaland.

Even though the British did not separate the region from Bengal Province it did give it a special status like the Backward Track Region, etc. at different points in time. However, this did not satisfy the aspirations of the people and the demand perpetually sustained itself. After independence the Hillmen’s Association ceased to exist and the onus of sustaining the movement was spearheaded by different groups. The movement lost its momentum after Independence and only after the 1980s did it resurface in a sustained and organized form. During the 1980s when the movement was violent and widespread, it failed to capture the imagination of the ‘national mainstream media’. Media, primarily print capitalism is considered to be an important medium through which nationalism and collective identity are forged, but not until the advent of social media and 24×7 news channels did the Gorkhaland movement receive any attention from the fourth pillar of democracy. This happened because of the marginalized status of Gorkhas in the national imagination.

HISTORY OF THE MEDIA IN DARJEELING

After taking over from the Rajah of Sikkim, the British shaped and moulded Darjeeling according to their imagination. They experimented with Darjeeling and helped in developing the infrastructure of the region. From the steam engine to the Hill Cart Road, the British built everything according to their requirements. Though the British were doing things for the better administration and sustenance of their rule, it needs to be acknowledged that they were instrumental in laying the foundation for the growth in the socio-political and economic consciousness of the people.

Numerous vernacular newspapers were published in Darjeeling due to the efforts of various individuals (Subba: 1992). The precursor to these publications were the English dailies and weeklies as well as monthly journals published by the British themselves. In 1892, many newspapers were in circulation in English. The Darjeeling News and Darjeeling Standard were published weekly. On the other hand, News and Notes was a monthly journal. Towards the end of the nineteenth century, between 1897 and 1898 there was a newspaper Darjeeling-Kalimpong-Sikkim News covering a wide area, and published every month (Darjeelinghistoryclub: 2020). The first monthly in the Nepali language Gorkha Bharat Jiwan was published in 1886 from Banaras and was edited by Motiram Bhatta (Subba: 1992). From the time Gorkha Bharat Jiwan came into circulation many other dailies, weeklies and monthlies were published from Darjeeling, Kalimpong, Kurseong and Dehradun till the first decade of the 1900. Some of the prominent magazines and periodicals published when Darjeeling was under the colonial rule were Gorkhey Khabar Kagat (1901), Suktisudha (1904), Madhavi (1906), Chandra (1914), Gorkhali (1915), Janmabhumi (1922), Himalayan Times (1926), Tarun Gorkha (1928), Adarsha (1930), Nepali Sahitya Sammelan (1932), Nebula and Parivartan (1934), Goan Sudhar (1939), Gorkha Samachar (1946) (Subba: 1992). There were some like the Gorkha Sevak (1935) published in northeastern parts of India, Gorkha Samachar was published in Delhi. Himalayan Times was edited by Saheb Narendrabikram and was in English. Adarsha a monthly periodical was the only one published in Nepali that discussed the financial situation and the economic activities of the people of Darjeeling Hills (Darjeelinghistoryclub: 2020). However, some of these had to be taken out of circulation due to many factors and one such factor was the lack of a sizeable readership and the other was the lack of entrepeneurship on the part of the editors to sell their products. The spread of the Nepali diaspora warranted the need for newspapers to shed light on the socio-economic and political conditions of the Nepali population hence there were numerous print media in circulation. However, the absence of a sizeable readership forced many of these periodicals to stop publication as it was not financially sustainable.

After independence there were few other newspapers and periodicals that emerged in Darjeeling and Kalimpong districts. Some of the prominent among them were the Himalayan Observer, The Swatantra Manchko Bulletin, Abo, Himali Bela, Gorkha, Ganthi Kura Dristikon and Himalchuli. Himalchuli was the only one which was still in circulation when the Gorkhaland agitation started in the 1980s. The writers in these papers and especially in Aba played a significant role in politically mobilizing the masses the beginning of the agitation in the 1980s (Subba: 1992). There was a paradigm shift in the writings of the early 1900s to the beginning of the mobilization for Gorkhaland agitation. These writings, however, did contribute in a latent manner in constructing the collective consciousness of the people converting them into a collectivity rather than just a joining together of discrete individuals without any common denominator. The writings which emerged after this period were more politically assertive and not as defensive as the earlier ones. The political imagination of the people by this time was already established due to the politics of Akhil Bharatiya Gorkha League (ABGL) and the pan-India Nepali Bhasa Andolan.1 The latter had especially contributed immensely to the forging of a strong collective consciousness of the Gorkha population. The freshly developed collective identity made people aware of the political, social and cultural capital they possessed and provided a sense of ownership of history, geography and their location within it. The collective strength gave them the belief that they could challenge all the wrong notions most of Indian people and the government had about Gorkha history and existence. The writings started questioning their being labelled as foreigners and by implication their citizenship. This was and still is one of the most important reasons for the demand for the creation of a separate state of Gorkhaland.

MEDIA AND SOCIAL MOVEMENT IN
DARJEELING REGION

As mentioned above the demand for separation from Bengal started in 1907. The political situation of Darjeeling then and when the movement erupted again in the 1980s under the leadership of the Gorkha National Liberation Front (GNLF) was completely different. However, the only thing that was common during these two historical periods was the conspicuous absence of the media. Media is a very essential component in social movement as it helps in propagating the issues, legitimizing the claims made by the movement and more importantly it helps in shaping the public perception and understanding of the political necessity of the movement (Rohlinger & Vaccaro: 2013). Though media plays an important role in social and political movements, it did not contribute anything to propagating the issues of the Gorkhaland movement at the national level.

By the time the movement started in the 1980s only the vernacular media was present. The absence of national dailies was visible. The only national daily that was in circulation was The Telegraph and it would not reach the people on time. At the earliest, it would reach the place only in the evening. It was more or less the same with The Statesman. The other national newspapers had negligible presence in the region. The above mentioned two newspapers that were in circulation among Gorkhas were not interested in disseminating the facts of the agitation, as the government had a firm grip on the news it was printing. Radio was another medium to reach people but being part of the governmental structure it did not play any significant role in disseminating and propagating the ideology of the movement. Moreover, radio was also not a commodity that was owned by the entire population which further restricted its efficacy. Even in post-independent India, media in Darjeeling was at its nebulous stage. It could not boast of a newspaper. The media had not yet found a location of its own in the lifeworld of the people. Media was still a component of the high culture and had not yet been part of the mass popular culture. In such a situation the only medium to disseminate and circulate information and news was the bhitta or the wall.

Every place had a designated bhitta for disseminating all kinds of news to the people. For the common people the bhitta for a long time had been a site for collective participation in the political discourse. Bhitta was the site for not just participation but also for dissent and protest and a medium for articulating one's political ideology and activism. People from all walks of life gathered to read the posters as well as dictates of political parties regarding what they thought of a particular development as well as their future course of action and the role the civil society and the community had to play. Standing in front of the bhitta and reading had been part of the everyday ritual for the reticent as well as the vocal political participant. People reading the news/announcement pasted in the bhitta would relay it to people in their locality and in this manner, the news spread far and wide. Bhitta was and is part of the cultural as well as political capital of the Gorkhas demand for Gorkhaland for over a century. Mainstream media in all its forms did not have a role to play during the Gorkhaland movement in the 1980s. The media was neither functional nor dysfunctional for the activists as well as the government. However, things were to change drastically when the movement started again in the new millennium.

Society and Change in Darjeeling

During the agitation, many lives were lost and property worth crores of rupees was damaged. It is believed that approximately 1,200 lives were lost from 1986 to 1988 when the agitation was at its peak. Atrocities and violence were inflicted by the state and central police forces among the people without any discrimination based on age or gender. The agitation came to an end when the Central Government, State Government and the GNLF came into an agreement and signed the tripartite agreement for the creation of Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council, a semi-autonomous body. However, one of the clauses in the agreement mentions that the agreement is the final settlement with regard to the demand of Gorkhaland, thereby putting an end to the aspiration of the people. This understanding created an ecosystem of animosity and the trust and support that GNLF enjoyed was gradually on the decline. GNLF was in power for almost three decades and in 2007 a reality show on television changed the political course of the region and the people.

The political dominance of the GNLF was challenged by one of its leaders, Bimal Gurung. He was able to garner the support of the people and it was very easy for him to gain the confidence of the people. GNLF had created a reign of terror and the demographic profile of the population had also undergone transformations. The young people who had witnessed the demand for Gorkhaland had internalized the importance of collective identity. In 2007 ‘Indian Idol’, the reality show became instrumental in bringing the Gorkha population spread across India as well as abroad in support of a Gorkha contestant in the show thus highlighting the collective strength of the Gorkhas. Solidarity among the people was once again at the forefront. Soon GNLF was overthrown by Gorkha Janmukti Morcha (GJM).

The GJM vehemently opposed the GNLF efforts to include the Darjeeling region under the Sixth Schedule until and unless the entire Gorkha community was included in the Scheduled Tribe list of India. The population in the hills has undergone not just quantitative but also qualitative change. The educational as well as economic status of the people had improved. The population had increased from 16.09 lakhs in 2001 to 18.47 lakhs in 2011 and the literacy rate had improved from 71.79 in 2001 to 79.56 in 2011 (Darjiling (Darjeeling) District Population Census 2011-2021-2023, West Bengal Literacy Sex Ratio and Density, n.d.). This population also had access to the media that had developed leaps and bounds since the 1980s.

The GJM was more fortunate than the GNLF as far as access to media was concerned. The prominent sources of media during the GJM-led agitation was Himalaya Darpan a local daily in Nepali published from Siliguri, the darjeelingtimes.com, and many cable television channels like the Hamro Channel, Himali Channel and Darjeeling Television. All these media outlets helped in the cause of GJM. Except Himalaya Darpan the proprietorship of the rest belonged to the natives of Darjeeling, Himalaya Darpan to establish itself took the cause of Gorkhaland and helped GJM in propagating their stories and news. GJM thus became successful in changing the narrative of the politics of the hills and once again the agitation for Gorkhaland erupted and again a tripartite agreement was signed for the creation of Gorkhaland Territorial Administration (GTA). Two differences emerged in the two agreements signed. First, it was the word Gorkhaland and second, the agreement on GTA acknowledges that the movement for Gorkhaland would sustain and carry on. With regard to polity, a very significant distinction emerged. GNLF and its leader, the Chairperson of the DGHC was seen as a puppet in the hands of the Government of West Bengal while GJM and its leader the Chief Executive Officer of GTA were cordial but was seen as indifferent of the State Government control. Bimal Gurung even led the masses when the Government did not fulfil its responsibilities towards the GTA. The media was supportive of his politics and assisted in taking his grievances against the government to the people thus making his ideas and narratives the dominant political discourse. These cable television networks had become so prominent in facilitating the connection and bonding between the people and the GJM that the District Magistrate, Darjeeling banned the telecast of cable television from 8 August to 11 August 2013, invoking the provisions of the Cable Television (Regulation) Amendment Act, 2011 (Dural: 2015).

The society in Darjeeling was witnessing rapid changes. The educational and economic status of the people were improving and many were now migrating to other places in search of better livelihood opportunities and to pursue educational aspirations. The migrant population later played a preemptive role in the movement.

Gorkhaland Movement in 2017

The relationship between the state government and the GTA was not stable and cordial, there were allegations and counter-allegations made by both parties. The GTA accused the state government of withholding transfer of departments that were listed in the agreement for the development of the region while the state government accused the GTA of embezzlement of development funds. Further, the GTA was not happy with the government for setting up Development Boards on the basis of community which they thought interfered with the functioning of the GTA. The political situation in the hills reflected the low point of the relationship between the two institutional actors, both manifesting their indignation against each other.

Particularly, the situation for the GJM, who was in power, was stable as the people were dissatisfied with the achievements and the state government was constantly accusing them of corruption and using it as a threat to discontinue the agreement. Under such a situation the West Bengal Government undertook the policy of implementing Bengal language in all the schools of the state. This was unacceptable to the people of Darjeeling and this policy implementation was perceived as language imperialism. Civil society and GJM were against the implementation of the Bengali language in place of the Nepali language. There was a protest not just from GJM but by all the Gorkha population living inside and outside of Darjeeling against this policy. Soon this gave way to the demand of Gorkhaland as it was thought that only the creation of Gorkhaland would put an end to the suppression and oppression experienced by the Gorkha people of India. During this phase of the movement, it was not just confined to Darjeeling hills alone but the demand for the creation of Gorkhaland was raised from many parts of the country.

The hills were closed for 107 days. There were hardships and troubles but the people were relentless in sustaining the bandh. Calls for the bandh to be withdrawn were made by the state and the central governments but it fell on deaf ears. The atrocities of the state became severe with every passing day. In no time 11 people were killed by the state apparatus. All the leaders of GJM were forced to go underground and it was common people who sustained the movement. The national media was mute to the happenings in the hills as usual. Only Republic Bharat reached Darjeeling to report the atrocities that people were facing. The Telegraph soon became the mouthpiece of the state government which was followed by the local vernacular Himalaya Darpan. These newspapers were printing only the government version of the story. Depending on the ideology of the owner’s television channels broadcasted news accordingly, either in favour or opposed to the state. News which till then had been more or less unbiased and objective now tended to be determined by political ideology. News became more of a propaganda tool for the government and the political order. Reality soon became what the news channels constructed to be and flooded the screen. All ideologies in the political spectrum started using television and news channels and only representational culture was given space in the news. News anchors gradually became agents of the representational culture denying space to alternative thoughts and dissenting voices.

Media and its Role During the Agitation

The agitation in 2017 was very different than the earlier ones. The leader of GJM, Bimal Gurung proclaimed in 2014 that henceforth all agitation will be peaceful according to the principles of Gandhi. The agitation in 2017 was also peaceful and the mode of dissent was to go on a strike, public rallies, street corner speeches and sloganeering. These actions were an endeavour to build the movement and the morale of all actors and also to communicate with them (Piven: 2008). However, the state apparatus was on the offensive and started using both the allocative and authoritative resources to show their power and to end the agitation. False cases were filed against all the leaders and the prominent associates of the leaders. Most of these people had more than 100 cases against them and many of them were also charged with the draconian Unlawful Activities Prevention Act. Such a situation forced all the leaders and their subordinates to hide leaving the onus of spearheading the movement to the lower cadres and the common people. The strike was sustained for 107 days till two leaders betrayed the movement and the people and forcefully withdrew the strike. There was no supply of necessary everyday requirements. People had to face tremendous hardship. Under these circumstances, there were few media which covered what was happening in the region but it soon faded out.

With the use of force and atrocities, the state was able to suppress everything and maintain its dominance in the region. The state censored all the news and the media fraternity could not get or report the actual happenings from the hills. Getting the word out on important news events from the region was such a challenge those days. In addition, countering distorted, biased and unverified news and narratives spewed by mainstream media and even social media platforms paid for by the state was almost impossible due to the lack of internet (The Centre for Internet and Society, n.d.). The hills were experiencing an existential crisis more than a political crisis as the tea gardens were closed and all form of communication was restricted by the state. The state could have acted in a humanitarian manner and showed its largess by allowing ration and other commodities to reach the people but it did not do so. The crisis in the region was much deeper than it could meet the eyes. Even in such a dire situation the media was propagating for the state and prophesizing the benefits the Chief Minister could accrue from the situation rather than reporting on the plight of the people. The Scroll wrote, ‘The agitation in West Bengal’s hill areas may end up benefitting the chief minister politically’ (Daniyal: 2017). The government used the media as an instrument to polarize public opinion. The media played its role as an instrument at the hands of the government by manufacturing false news those were detrimental to the movement. The agitation in 2017 was coordinated by the Gorkhaland Movement Coordination Committee (GMCC) comprising members from all the parties but one of the media based on the opinion of a government official reported, ‘It is clear from the decisions taken during the all-party meeting in the hills that the Morcha leadership has started compromising with the demands of other hill parties. This is a clear hint that the Morcha leadership is weakening’ (Scoopwhoop: 2017).

Social Media and the Gorkhaland Agitation

The setting in of the vernacular marked a new beginning in terms of the dissemination of opinions and news. It offered a new platform for many to understand, articulate, reflect and reflect on the political economy of the region and to an extent of those nearby places dominated by the Gorkha population. It also assisted in forging a relation among the imagined community giving a new but desired traction to the common cause of identity and the predominant politics associated with it. Furthermore, the dailies also led to the beginning of modernity and the establishment of a community, that connected by sharing their concerns and opinions about the urgent issues of the community. The dailies also helped in bringing modern technology and technological developments closure to the people. Soon technology became common which further pushed the community into a new direction of modernity and information. By the time the 2017 agitation started internet became a source of all development social, political and economic (Navarria: 2019). Internet in the guise of social media filled the vacuum that a biased and prejudiced or passive media had left.

Social media platforms like Facebook, Twitter, WhatsApp, and Instagram were all proactively used by the people to talk and write about the movement as well as the police atrocities. The activism of the people through these platforms helped in galvanizing support from those who were not in Darjeeling. The Telegraph reported on this aspect of the movement and the government was quick to ban the internet. In the absence of the Internet, it was difficult for the movement activists to synchronize and execute their plans. The use of social media helped the common people to organize themselves and connect (Lopes: 2014) and establish a pro-movement narrative. ‘The possibility to access, recombine and distribute knowledge freely or with minimum restriction can help subvert established hegemonic power positions by providing new entry points to the political process for those actors usually relegated in a non-active position by the predominant influence of existing power-holders over mainstream media networks’ (Navarria: 2019).

The use of social media helped the movement in taking the ground realities to people across the globe. With the help of social media, the movement gained momentum and protests and demonstrations were held not just in Darjeeling and other places in the country but across different countries where even a handful of people from Darjeeling were residing. There was coordination among activists from different places and also from other countries which helped in ‘gaining regional and global resonance in diverse audiences across the world’ (Porta & Mattoni: 2015, 53).

Another very important dimension of the movement was the use of WhatsApp. WhatsApp helped the activists to construct for themselves an autonomous communication, which very essential for the movement to relate with the society (Castells: 2015). This platform was used by the activists as well as the leaders to connect and to discuss and propose ideas that were important for the movement in which the main actors were missing from the scene. The multiple features of WhatsApp like voice messages and the facility to use languages other than English also helped greatly as all activists were not well versed in the English language. The voice message facility was used to the fullest. The leaders would also use this to pass on the messages in tones that were effective in arousing the sensibilities of the activists towards the movement. The activists in turn would forward these messages to the general public. The act of forwarding the messages would ensure that the message would reach the entire population. WhatsApp was very instrumental as well as successful as a medium to sustain the connection between the leaders, the activists and the general population.

The leaders who had betrayed the movement and the people had by coercion and use of state repressive apparatus usurped power and were in control of the GTA. The movement activists were hounded by the state and the police. In such a situation the general election of 2019 was pertinent to show the support that the movement and GJM enjoyed from the people. Along with WhatsApp, it was also Google Meet that played a very significant role in planning and executing the plans. According to the Secretary and President of GJM if it had not been for Google Meet and WhatsApp it would not have been possible for them to organize participation in the election. The candidate was discussed over Google Meet and WhatsApp and approval from all the stakeholders was sought. There were over 100 meetings with the use of Google Meet among party cadres hiding at different locations. The candidate for the election was also decided using these platforms. The significance of WhatsApp can be analysed from the fact that according to one of the parties functionaries, the candidate was decided at around two at night in consultation with the alliance party. All the members of the party were informed through WhatsApp immediately but by six in the morning, the candidate had to be changed as the cadres had disapproved of the candidate who was again replaced by another candidate.

Apart from the core party members making use of these platforms, they also used these platforms to generate support for the issue of Gorkhaland. Numerous Facebook pages like the Darjeeling Chronicles and Gorkhaland Flag Campaign started functioning as the spokesperson of the movement. Pro-movement information and activities in any form started being shared on these pages. The ordinary citizens who were witnessing the making of history were using their mobile phones to record (Castells: 2015). Everybody with a smartphone participating in the movement thus inadvertently became a broadcaster (Porta & Mattoni: 2015) of events which was of interest and importance for the movement and could transfer to these pages for the consumption of wider audiences. They also ridiculed the leaders who had betrayed the movement and went against the ruling party in West Bengal. These Facebook pages were instrumental in communicating with the masses and disseminating and propagating the importance of Gorkhaland and the agitation for preserving the distinct identity and cultural heritage of the region and community. They became an effective tool and the government reacted to their influence and impact by filing many false cases against them to the extent that one of the editors of Darjeeling Chronicles could not even attend his father’s funeral. Internet in the hills during the 2017 agitation and after became a medium which assisted the public and activists alike to organize and execute their everyday interactions (Fuchs: 2015) ‘in ways beyond the control of the state’ (Salem: 2015, 172).

Apart from acting in a repressive manner the government and its agencies also was proactive in surveillance. The police and other state machinery had completely overtaken the public spaces and had established their grip with the help of those who had betrayed the movement. The government tried to change the political discourse and the dominant narrative with repression at the earlier stage of the movement and when this did not have the desired effect, they tried to counter the narrative with the help of social media. The government in this endeavour was assisted by those who had joined hands with the leaders who had betrayed the movement and those who were against GJM. They also started using Facebook pages to propagate misinformation about the movement particularly about the GJM and Bimal Gurung. They would run an online voting campaign and the results would always be the candidate of the opposition winning the online voting. Along with these tactics, the agency hired by the ruling party of West Bengal to strategize their election campaign offered monetary and other benefits to the platforms that were pro-Gorkhaland. The government tried to use its agents to polarize the population through the use of these platforms and establish a narrative which showed that they were good and the movement actors were bad (Lukito et al.: 2022).

CONCLUSION

The Gorkhaland movement can be traced to 1907 when for the first time a memorandum to the then Viceroy of Bengal Presidency was submitted. Since then, the separation from Bengal has been sought after by the people of the Darjeeling region. However, this separation has still not been achieved even after more than 115 years. It is well known that media plays an important role in social movements as these movements try to ‘communicate their grievances’ and ‘build the movement and its morale’ (Piven: 2008, 23) and also communicate with the larger public. In the case of the Gorkhaland agitation due to its long history, different forms of media have come into being and the direction in which the movement has been reflected is not uniform. In the initial period, the media and the writings on it were more subdued and apologetic. In the 1980s the media became more offensive and aggressive while vehemently defending the demand and rubbishing the foreigner tag that was labelled against the Gorkha population. But this was only in the vernacular press and did not contribute much to change the perception and narrative that was dominant at the national level.

In the new millennium when the entire world was experiencing a boom in information technology, Darjeeling was readying itself to embrace this social change. There was socio-economic improvement in the people taking place and many had also socialized to this new way of life. In 2007 when the agitation again started internet became the tool for manifesting the grievances that lay dormant for years under the rule of GNLF. After a hiatus of another decade when the agitation started in 2017 the use of the internet was at the core of the movement. The press and television were to a large extent under the control of the government. Therefore, social media was the only resource in the hands of the people to fight the repression and atrocities of the state and its apparatuses collectively. Any form of physical gathering or collectivity was not possible due to the brutality of the state. Social media helped people to collectively raise pertinent issues and make one another aware of what the state was doing. Internet and social media have provided an alternative to mobilization and have provided a platform where individuals can perform different roles. They can manifest their power by breaking rules and interacting with many other like-minded actors. Social media provided a space where people could exercise their agency not just individually but also collectively without being identified by the authorities and making them victims of their brutality. Social media empowered common people in their fight against the state by providing them an identity of anonymity.

Anonymity gave people the courage to act against the combination of all state actors along with the leaders who had betrayed the movement and their team but at the same time, it did not all the time facilitate sameness and oneness of activities carried by different individuals. This anonymity also acted as an impediment towards the movement as many opposed to the movement and the leadership using the internet and social media also propagated—anti-movement narratives. However, Darjeeling being a small region and with not so big population it was not very difficult for the state to at least roughly identify the users as they controlled to internet service providers. On the one hand, the nexus between the corporates and the state became detrimental to achieving effective results on the other social media has provided a viable space for non-state actors to challenge the dominance and hegemony of the state (Trottier & Fuchs: 2015).

Darjeeling during the agitation witnessed an increase in the use of social media. GJM was successful in using it to fulfil its political objective by mobilizing people and passing its message across the people. This helped the people to realize the potential power of collective identity and solidarity. As a consequence, they were able to win election in the 2019 general elections and prove that the people were in support of their demand for Gorkhaland. Most importantly the use of social media was instrumental in raising sensibilities towards the movement and one another. From being defensive in the media in the earlier phase to actively partial writings in the 1980s, the use of social media in the agitation of 2017 and after, helped the leaders and most importantly, the majority of the people to be legally, constitutionally and socially erudite and fight the state in their just and rightful demand of separation from the state of West Bengal.
