Foreword

In the intricate tapestry of South Asia, the threads of history, politics and human security are interwoven to create a vibrant mosaic. Conflict, Peace and Human Security in South Asia is a timely and significant contribution to the understanding of this region’s ongoing transformation. This book casts light on pertinent issues with a multifaceted exloration of the challenges and opportunities that lie within the tapestry.

The book opens with an insightful examination of Nepal’s political parties, their ideological conviction and democratization of Nepal. It then transitions to the post-conflict political participation of women in Nepal, highlighting the strides made towards gender equality in the political arena—a testament to the resilience and empowerment of Nepalese women.

As we delve deeper, the book addresses the empowerment of crime victims within the Nepali legal framework, reflecting on the nation’s journey toward justice and the rule of law. The stability of the democratic constitutions is then scrutinized, underscoring the importance of robust foundations for the sustenance of democracy.

The narrative continues with an analysis of fragmented unions and the social facets of living together in the contemporary societics. It then presents an insight on the Buddhist principles that have shaped the nation’s legal and moral compass since mid-nineteenth century. The plight of Bhutanese asylum seekers in Nepal is poignantly portrayed, revealing the cost of the quest for identity and belongings.

China’s role in South Asia is critically assessed, considering the economic, geopolitical and cultural implications of its growing influence. This discussion is particularly relevant in the context of the shifting power dynamics within the region and the broader international political landscape.

Each chapter is a window into the soul of Nepal and South Asia, reflecting the region’s diversity, its struggles for peace and security and the indomitable spirit of its people. This book is not just a scholarly discourse but a narrative of human experiences, aspirations and the relentless pursuit of harmonious existence.

As we turn pages, we are invited to engage with the ideas and research presented, to question, to reflect and to contribute to the ongoing dialogue about the future of South Asia. I hope that this book will serve as a treatise and a resource for policymakers, academics and everyone interested in South Asia.

I congratulate Dr. Purna Bahadur Karki for his meticulous editorship, and every author for their scholarly contribution.

2 June 2024

DR. MANOJ KUMAR SHARMA

Justice

Supreme Court of Nepal
