Acknowledgements

The completion of this book is like a long sea journey. Although at times, I felt all by myself, steering my tiny nutshell through storm and bad weather, I was really surrounded by ever so many good forces working towards a safe passage. In the course of my research on textile trade of the Tamil coast with the Portuguese, Dutch, French and English, I came across some documents on slave trade. I continued to look for more and accumulated substantial material during my visits to many archives. I heard the voices of the slaves through the data when I wrote, but it took a long time to complete since I continued to unearth as many documents as possible. The historical records do not remain flat or lack nuance, but give individual expression and emotion, making it easy to understand the journey made by slaves through several agents, brokers, private merchants and East India Companies. This work illustrates the inspiring and interesting study on European slave trading in the Tamil Coast with the markets around the globe and reconstructs the world of human beings who were held captive for an indefinite period of time, coerced into extremely dependent and exploitative power relationships, denied rights (including rights over their labour, lives and bodies) and often vulnerable to forced relocation by various means and forced to labour against their will. The selection of the chapters explains to a large extent, the transcultural, transcontinental and transnational interactions in broad global historical perspective.

I have incurred enormous debts to the generosity, interest, guidance and friendship of a significant number of organizations. As with any project on transoceanic networks, mine was reliant on a global community of support, encouragement and expertise.
