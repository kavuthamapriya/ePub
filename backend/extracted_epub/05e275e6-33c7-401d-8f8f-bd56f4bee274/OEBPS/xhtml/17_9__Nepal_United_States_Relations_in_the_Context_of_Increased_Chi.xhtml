INTRODUCTION

Nepal and the United States established diplomatic relations in 1948. The US has been helping to support Nepal in promoting political stability and economic development and providing humanitarian assistance. Furthermore, the US assists Nepal through bilateral relations for peace and security. Globally, the US and Nepal support each other to achieve both nations’ policies and objectives in the international community. Nepal is becoming one of the most important countries to US policy to obtain South Asian security and monitor Chinese and Indian militarism and nuclear security, as well as Pakistani extremism. In contemporary discourse, the presence of the US in South Asia also impacts the global influence of the US.

Historically and in the literature, the Second World War, the Cold War, the post-Cold War era, the aftermath of 9/11, and China’s emergence since 2004-5 have all highlighted the importance of South Asia and the Indian Ocean in maintaining and sustaining the superpower image and influence of the US in Asia. When examined, the US policy pursued in South Asia and West Asia during Second World War and the Cold War shows that Sri Lanka supported US interests in countering Russian presence in the region. This indicates how important South Asia is in the US policy and stability programmes, not regional considerations but also for controlling communism and reinforcing but also for controlling communism and reinforcing the US superpower in contemporary Asia.

Additionally, when US policy toward South Asia indirectly was evaluated and observed, through Japan and India, as proxies of the US that implement US policy. Japan has represented the US ever since 9/11 to revitalize and activate policy against global terrorism. The US has sought loyal partners in South Asia and India is a reliable partner just like Japan.

China will collide with the US. It is emerging as an economic powerhouse and is spontaneously threatening US interests, influence and even position in South Asia. China is challenging the superpower image and influence of the US. This first took place in South Asia, where traditional allies of the US, such as Pakistan, Sri Lanka and the Maldives are looking to China for economic and development assistance.

In addition to its political, economic and security interests, China is in search of new markets for investment. China is focusing on giving assistance, such as long-term loans and expertise to South Asian nations, such as Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Nepal and has actively engaged in the region. China proposed the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank, which will enhance the position of China if it becomes active. This will reduce the influence of the US and Japan. Given these conditions, the US must review its policy program to challenge the Chinese presence.

In its development perspective and policy ‘One Belt and One Road’ (OBOR), China proposes establishing and creating a network of railways, roads, pipelines and utility grid that would link China and other central Asian nations, which would pave the way for connecting China and the world. This would boost the Chinese involvement in this region, as well as providing a platform for economic cooperation, including policy coordination, trade and financing collaboration and social and cultural cooperation. This would enhance the Chinese initiative towards a leadership position in the world forum, where recently US policy and programmes have worked against the member countries. Also, Trump’s policy of ‘America First’ opens the US to more criticism in international relations, which might be problematic for sustaining a US leadership role in the world forum.

Further, in the future, how China builds its military power against traditional American allies will determine the superpower image of the US in the region. Moreover, recent developments involving regional tension and allies, as well as national internal conflicts, have gone against American interests. In this situation, the American policy programme is important in Asia, where China, North Korea, South Korea, Philippines and Japan are all suffering from internal conflicts involving security and disputes in the South China Sea.

Recently, the media, politicians and academics have debated the leadership change in the US government in the Trump era in both positive and negative ways. Trump’s bilateralism may provide more opportunities for the American people and may build stronger bonds with the international community. However, some fear that extreme policies might reduce US global power. But not only it gives the US a clear road to power but also provides security for the US. In this chapter, the following hypotheses are tested:

(i) Trump’s bilateralism policy will have good/ bad implications for stability in Asia.

(ii) Nepal can play an important role as an intermediary between the US and China.

Further, explorations regarding relations between the US and Nepal in the context of China’s increased influence in South Asia during the Trump era is done.

METHODOLOGY

This research employed a multifaceted approach to data collection and analysis, with a strong emphasis on qualitative methods. Secondary data were gathered through an extensive literature review and systematic comparison. To gain a comprehensive understanding of contemporary discourse concerning Nepal and the United States, as well as their politico-economic and societal contexts, qualitative aspects of social components were meticulously examined. Additionally, historical analysis was conducted to contextualize these findings within a broader temporal framework.

NEPAL AND INDIA RELATIONS IN THE CONTEXT
OF INCREASED CHINESE INFLUENCE
IN SOUTH ASIA

Nepal and India have cordial bilateral relations and they share common cultural elements, traditions, and religion. According to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs on Nepal-India relations, it states that the two countries established diplomatic relations on 17 June 1947. In principles of peaceful coexistence, sovereign equality and understanding of each other’s aspiration and sensitivities, Nepal and India not only have relations through treaties and agreements between the two countries but also have people-to-people contact and free movements of people between the countries. At the political level, Nepal and India have good relations, with frequent high-level visits by the leaders between the two countries. Recently, for example, Prime Ministers exchanged visits within a short period. This illustrates the importance of India-Nepal relations at the political level.

Looking back to Nepalese leaders’ state visits to India, it indicates that almost all Nepali political leaders and Prime Minsters visited India after they assumed office. Reciprocally, an invitation from Nepal’s then Prime Minister, Sushil Koirala, led to an official visit to Nepal in August 2014, by the Prime Minister of India, His Excellency Shri Narendra Modi. This was a historic visit by a prime minister of India to Nepal, after 17 years, which reflected the commitment of the newly-formed government in India to enhance India’s relations with neighbouring countries (Ministry of Foreign Affair, Nepal, 2017. This indicates the importance of Nepal relations to India after the change of leadership in India in response to China’s increasing development assistance and engagement in this region.

In relation to the Prime Minster of India’s third visit on Nepal within a four-years’ period, Thapa (2018) states, ‘India has realized by now that the use of coercive diplomacy severely dented bilateral relations. Therefore, India wants to revamp its image in Nepal and Modi’s reciprocal visit within a month of Oli’s visit to India signals the urge and necessity to do so’. Also, Bagachi Indrani (2018) states that it is ‘to reset ties’. Analysis of the state level visits and Indian engagements indicates that its diplomatic course and policy are shifting towards Nepal. A review of ongoing engagements of Nepal’s friends to Nepal’s development and their assistance and aid reflect that China-Nepal-India trilateral relations are becoming more viable than before.

Further, when the political parties and their ideology are considered, Nepal’s and India’s governments have different political ideology but Nepal and China have communist governments. A basic assumption is that Nepal faces challenges in maintaining balanced relations with both neighbours. The perception is that, due to its communist government, some scholars and others predict that Nepal will incline more toward China, which India would perceive as a security threat to Indian security interests. However, it is arguable that, whether governments have the same ideology or not, China always supports those who are in government, as well as their decisions, rather than following communist ideology. On the other hand, India’s diplomacy is now slightly changed and has a political tone. Moreover, the cultural factors and open borders are strong influences, so that in the future Nepal, India and China relations will be stronger than in the past. However, one cannot ignore that China and India both have an interest in Nepal and that both nations want to make their presence felt in Nepal.

For Nepal, India has been the key development partner. India had been supporting Nepal in developing projects and political affairs during the insurgency and time of mass movements. India has been assisting Nepal in different sectors through economic assistance to Nepal, such as infrastructure development, capacity development of human resources and initiation of large and intermediate project that have been undertaken with Indian economic assistance. Nepal does not have direct contact to seaports, so its trade depends mostly on the Indian market. India and Nepal have a bilateral treaty on transit, a treaty on trade and agreements of cooperation to control unauthorized trade. The foregoing provides both national investment and trade facilities and provide Nepal with transit facilities to support third country trade.

For China and Nepal, due to geographical structure and complication, there are not many options or routes for trade and transit points between Nepal and China. Only the Rasuwagadhi-Kerung border trade and transit point has been updated to international standards after the great earthquake which damaged the transit point and closed border link roads.

Since the earthquake and the blockade along the Nepal-India border in September 2015, Nepal has become closer to China as it has tried to find alternatives to trade and transit to India, allowing China to step into the Nepali market that was, in past, India’s monopoly. Further, according to the diplomat newspaper through the ‘One Belt, One Road’ initiative, Beijing is looking into the possibility of connecting Kathmandu to Lhasa in Tibet via railways at an estimated cost of $8 billion. China’s activities have been steadily expanding in Nepal after Kathmandu’s support for OBOR materialized. This happened despite India’s stiff resistance to ‘OBOR’. So, for both neighbours, Nepal is an important friend for not only their economic interests but also in multilateral and regional fora.

China’s engagement in South Asia and Nepal contributes to more assertive diplomacy and friendship between Nepal and India. Meanwhile, both neighbours and Nepal’s political parties are reluctant to act based on their ideology but Nepal’s government aims for a balanced foreign policy towards its neighbours. According to the Constitution of Nepal, foreign policy of Nepal is guided by abiding faith in the United Nations and the policy of non-alignment. It includes:

• Mutual respect for each other’s territorial integrity and sovereignty.

• Non-interference in each other’s internal affairs.

• Respect for mutual equality.

• Non-aggression and the peaceful settlement of disputes.

• Cooperation for mutual benefit.

Contact between the people of China and Nepal is restricted. Both countries require visa, unlike with India and Nepal. However, the relations of Nepal and India are cordial, with bilateral agreements. Considering the social and cultural bonds that both countries have in common, it can be said that they are like family. Both countries have people-to-people relations that are stronger than state level, such that the countries are culturally and socially connected.

CHINESE ACTIVISM IN SOUTH ASIA AND
TRADITIONAL US INFLUENCE

In the twenty-first century, South Asia has experienced many changes caused by internal and external factors that affected the political stability of South Asian region. Since the 9/11 terrorist attacks, world politics and country polarization have occurred globally, impacting South Asia. This divided nations into two sides concerning the global war against terrorism. US had influence around the world, making India and US a strategic partnership against terrorism and in global issues, which further made India an emerging US ally in South Asian region. In the international forum and among South Asian national issues, the US has tried to view Nepal and other South Asian countries through the Indian perspective, depending on Indian analysis. This strengthened Indian and US influence on South Asian issues in politics, human rights and international cooperation.

Recently, however, the change in leadership in the US and India has had direct effects on the politics and regional alliances and structures. For the structure of power balance in contemporary Asia, India, China, the US and Japan all play vital roles for stability and peace in this region. Leadership that is transformational, reformative and constructive is needed, however, US President Trump’s bilateralism policy has already distanced the US. from global and multilateral alliances, which can jeopardy the US global superpower image. This already shows effects on trade, immigration and political fora. In international relations, the US is losing its hegemony. This distancing from the global alliances, resulting from the rejection of the Trans-Pacific Partnership (TPP), the pull-out from the Paris Climate Accords, Jagannathan (2017) places the US at odds with the G7 on an agreement which 195 nations have signed and 147 have formally ratified. This set back the US global superpower image and hegemony in international relations. In the case of South Asian countries, the US had previously expressed their political interest through their alliances, such as with Japan and India, in terms of economics assistance and support and cooperation in global fora.

In contrast to the actions of the US, China is expanding its presence in South Asia and in international relations. In South Asia, China is developing good relations with countries, such as Sri Lanka, Pakistan and Maldives and offering economic assistance without any conditions. As a result, countries that traditionally relied on Indian and US assistance are now accepting Chinese support. Thus, China’s trade and growing engagement on this region is bringing other South Asian states under China’s influence. China’s engagement in Pakistan, for example, with investment in infrastructure projects and collaborations on security and defence, indicate its growing presence in this region.

In the context of possible policy options for Nepal in response to both growing Chinese and US influence and the future consequences of Trump’s policy of bilateralism, Nepal can gain advantages from both India and China. Under Trump’s policies, Nepal might not continue to get assistance that the US had previously given and as a result suffers. Although Nepal can propose, on its own, conditions in international fora to support US policy which benefit Nepal. However, the current situation is that the US and India are trying to influence Nepal’s close relationship and policies towards China, considering these as threats for their superpower image in areas such as refugee issues and human rights issues.

In the context of India and China, there is a significant possibility that both nations will be inclined to support the development of Nepali projects. This inclination is driven by their competitive efforts to establish Nepal as a strategic ally.

CONCLUSION

After the World War Two, the US highlighted its national interest and that became a globalized priority through an emerging new world order. This US policy focused on developing an international order based on economic interdependence and multilateralism and strategic alliances under US leadership. This promoted US leadership and hegemony. Recent changes in leadership and bilateralism show the US distancing itself from global fora. This may cause instability, risk the security of its allies and have a negative impact on globalization. Although, for some, this gives a positive interpretation to the ‘America First’ slogan, it positions nationalism first, whereas, in this century, almost all elements are interconnected and globalized, requiring multilateralism.

In the larger Asia, South Asia is important, with China, India and Pakistan all nuclear countries. China’s activism in South Asia gives it an opportunity to lead the world in terms of trade with India and South Asian states, which gives control of a large market. China’s trade with the US and the African continent and its participation in international fora, give China increased influence, particularly considering the US pulling out of international collaborations and commitments.

India and China can contribute in a major way to the stability and development of the South Asian region. In the long term, US traditional relations and influence on security, as well as the US Indian strategic partnership, are still viable, due to Pakistani and Chinese factors. The US India strategic relationship plays a critical role to check the balance between India, China and Pakistan.

Nepal-India relations, relative to increased Chinese influence, provide the possibility of more options for Nepal and India in economic cooperation, with both countries benefiting.
