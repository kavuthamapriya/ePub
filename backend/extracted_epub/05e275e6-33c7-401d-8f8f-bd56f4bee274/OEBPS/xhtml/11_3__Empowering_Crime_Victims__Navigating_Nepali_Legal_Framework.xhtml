CHAPTER 3

Empowering Crime Victims:
Navigating Nepali Legal
Framework

SHOVA PATHAK

INTRODUCTION

The traditional criminal justice system pays very little attention to the victims of crime. For Salmond the purpose of criminal justice is punishment. We can regard it as a method of protecting society by reducing the occurrence of criminal behaviour or else we can consider it as an end. Punishment can protect society deterring potential offender and by reforming and turning him into a law-abiding citizen.1

Not only criminal justice system of states but also the international law paid sufficient attention to crime victims. Carlos Fernandez de Casadevante Romani wrote, ‘As it is well known and owing to the predominantly interstate structure of the international community, international norms are created to respond to states’ interests and goal. In this context attention to persons or individuals by states has only taken place in some field of international law.’2 According to him in case with human rights, international criminal law or international humanitarian law, the way in which the victims are considered differs.3

In human rights law, victims are acknowledged when the state is the author of the breach of an international obligation, but this branch of international law does not consider the breach of international obligations by non-state actors.4 In international criminal law and international humanitarian law on the other hand individuals may be regarded as victims as a consequence of acts perpetrated by individuals as well as by non-state actors.5 Therefore, international community has not yet codified binding international norms generally applicable to the victims of all types of crimes.

The victims have certain rights which must be protected to ensure that they get a fair treatment from the state agencies in the criminal justice process. Their concerns and needs must be responded sensitively by informing them about the progress of the case and by minimizing their miseries, offering protection and security to them and their families against any intimidation and retaliation. The police require the cooperation and support from the victim to prepare the case for prosecution in addition to tracing and apprehending the offender. Therefore, the protection of the crime victim is not only the protection offered to them but the protection of criminal justice and the whole justice system.

MEANING AND DEFINITION OF
CRIME VICTIM

‘Crime victim’ is a person who has been physically, financially or emotionally injured and/or had their property taken or damaged by someone committing a crime. Traditionally, the term crime victim has been defined as an individual who has been directly subjected to criminal activity.6 This definition of crime victim excludes the family members or defendant of the person who died because of crime. German criminologist Hans Joachim Schneider states that a victim can be an individual, an organization, ‘the moral order’ or the legal system of a state which is threatened, harmed, or destroyed by an action.7 The Declaration of Basic Principles of Justice for Victims of Crime and Abuse of Power defines crime victim in the following words:

‘Victims’ means persons who, individually or collectively, have suffered harm, including physical or mental injury, emotional suffering, economic loss or substantial impairment of their fundamental rights, through acts or omissions that are in violation of criminal laws operative within Member States, including those laws proscribing criminal abuse of power.8

Crime Victim Protection Act, 2075 (2018) has provided three types of victims namely First Degree Victim, Second Degree Victim and Family Victim in the form of a natural person. First Degree Victim means a person who as a result of the offender’s age, mental illness, diplomatic immunity or professional immunity does not have to bear criminal liability or the offender’s identity is unknown or charge is not made against the offender or the case related to the crime is taken back or the punishment imposed to the offender is pardoned or conviction is not made or regardless of the relation between the victim and offender, death occurred as a direct result of the crime commit detains the victim or loss is suffered and the term shall also indicate the person who dies or suffers loss while not engaging in crime in any of the following conditions:

1. While acting to stop the person from committing the crime.

2. While acting to provide reasonable support and rescue, in conditions when crime is being committed against a person, with an objective to prevent such a person from crime.

3. While attempting to arrest a person who is committing or has committed a crime or while providing help to an authorized person in course of arresting a suspicious, accused or convicted person.

Likewise, ‘Second Degree Victim’ means a person who is not involved in the crime committed or being committed against the First Degree Victim but as a result of being an eyewitness incurred loss and this term shall also mean a person not involved in the crime but has information of the crime or a guardian of a First Degree Minor Victim who incurred loss as a result of being an eyewitness and the term shall also mean the following persons who incurred loss as a result of having knowledge of the crime committed against the First Degree Victim:

(i) Guardian of First Degree Victim,

(ii) in case the First Degree Victim is a minor, and

(iii) in case the person incurring such loss is not involved in such crime.

‘Family Victim’ means the mother, father, husband, wife in consanguinity with the dead victim or any other person in the consanguinity family dependent on the victim who is not involved in the crime committed against the First Degree Victim as a direct consequence of which he or she died.

VICTIM OF CRIME UNDER CRIMINAL
JUSTICE SYSTEM

A justice system depends upon evidence against the case; they are appropriately being collected and presented before the courts. And, particularly, if fear prevails, collection of evidence is very difficult or not at all. The absence of evidence may finally lead to not taking up the cases or dismiss the charges against the accused, as the court can only consider on the matters brought before the court. This will lead to the space to weaken the case or escape the real culprits/perpetrators or lose the case.

Silencing victims through fear is one of the worst forms of impunity. It maintains the illusion of the rule of law and a legal system capable of following up wrongdoing. If any, complaints are made and if they are, they rarely result in prosecutions or convictions. Silence denies the existence of the problem; the problem may be known but is never spoken about.9 One of the challenges for many countries is in obtaining the cooperation of victims and witnesses to obtain important information and evidence about the criminal matters.10

The Declaration of Basic Principles of Justice for Victims of Crime and Abuse of Power, 1985, recognizes four basic rights of crime victims as:

• The right of access to trial proceedings and fair treatment.

• The right to compensation by the perpetrator.

• The right to compensation by the state.

• The right to necessary material, medical, psychological and social assistance from volunteer and public institutions.

The Constitution of Nepal drafted by the Constituent Assembly has guaranteed the right of crime victim including right to justice and compensation, for the first time in the world. The statutes drafted thereafter including the Muluki Criminal Code, 2017 and Muluki Criminal Procedure Code, 2017 and the Sentencing Act, 2017 have incorporated important provisions regrading the right of crime victims. Recently, the parliament has enacted comprehensive law for the protection of crime victim in 2018. In the later sections, the provisions of national law regarding crime victims including constitutional provisions, the provisions of general laws and specific legal provisions on rights to crime victim will be discussed in detail.

CONCEPT OF RIGHTS OF VICTIMS
OF CRIME

The word ‘right’ generally means a claim or an interest provided by the law or rights recognized and protected by law. The rights of crime victims mean the rights or claim of the victim of crime in the criminal justice process when they are a direct or an indirect victim. According to the US Code SS 3771(1) there are the following rights:

1. The right to be reasonably protected from the accused.

2. The right to reasonable, accurate and timely notice of any public court proceeding, or any parole proceeding, involving the crime or of any release or escape of the accused.

3. The right not to be excluded from any such public court proceeding, unless the court, after receiving clear and convincing evidence, determines that testimony by the victim would be materially altered if the victim hears other testimony at that proceeding.

4. The right to be reasonably heard at any public proceeding in the district court involving release, plea, sentencing or any parole proceeding.

5. The reasonable right to confer with the attorney for the Government in the case.

6. The right to full and timely restitution as provided in law.

7. The right to proceedings free from unreasonable delay.

8. The right to be treated with fairness and with respect for the victim’s dignity and privacy.

9. The right to be informed in a timely manner of any plea bargain or deferred prosecution agreement.

10. The right to be informed of the rights under this section and the services described in section 503(c) of the Victims’ Rights and Restitution Act of 1990 [42 U.S.C. 10607(c)]/[1] and provided contact information for the Office of the Victims’ Rights Ombudsman of the Department of Justice.11

According to the Declaration of Basic Principles of Justice for Victims of Crime and Abuse of Power, 1985,

• The right of access to trial proceedings and fair treatment.

• The right to compensation by the perpetrator.

• The right to compensation by the state.

• The right to necessary material, medical, psychological and social assistance from volunteer and public institutions

THE RIGHT TO ACCESS TO JUSTICE
AND FAIR TREATMENT

The crime victim thus receives the right to respect and acknowledgements at all levels of the criminal proceedings that is they have right to access the mechanism of justice and to prompt redress for the harm they have suffered. They have the right to receive continual information about how the case is proceeding, the right to give information to those responsible for decisions regarding the criminal, access to legal counsel, protection of personal integrity and physical security and the right to economic compensation.

RIGHT TO RESTITUTION

Restitution means payment made to the victim for damage suffered because of crime. It is intended to repay victims for their losses caused by the crime. The Declaration on the Rights of Victim emphasized on the fair restitution to the victim of crime. According to the paragraph 8, offenders or third parties responsible for their behaviour should, where appropriate, make fair restitution to victims, their families or dependents. Such restitution should include the return of property or payment for the harm or loss suffered, reimbursement of expenses incurred because of the victimization, the provision of services and the restoration of rights.

RIGHT TO COMPENSATION

It is the duty of the state to protect the life and property of people. The consequence of the state’s failure should not be borne by the people. This matter has been recognized by the declaration. The declaration provides that when compensation is not fully available from the offender or other sources, States should endeavour to provide financial compensation to the victims who have sustained significant bodily injury or impairment of physical or mental health as a result of serious crimes; the family, in particular dependents of persons who have died or become physically or mentally incapacitated as a result of such victimization.

RIGHT TO ASSISTANCE

Crime victims require assistance in various aspects including medical, psychological and social assistance for their physical protection as well as for successful prosecution against the wrongdoer. According to paragraphs 14 to 17 of the declaration, the victims should receive the necessary material, medical, psychological and social assistance through governmental, voluntary, community-based and indigenous means. They should be informed of the availability of health and social services and other relevant assistance and be readily afforded access to them. Police, justice, health, social service and other personnel concerned should receive training to sensitize them to the needs of victims and guidelines to ensure proper and prompt aid. In providing services and assistance to victims, attention should be given to those who have special needs.

RIGHTS OF CRIME VICTIMS UNDER
NEPALI LEGAL FRAMEWORK

CONSTITUTIONAL PROVISION ON THE
RIGHTS OF CRIME VICTIMS

Nepali criminal justice system has taken a big leap in the protection of the rights of crime victim in recent years Constitution of Nepal made by the Constituent Assembly has guaranteed the right of the crime victim for the first time. Article 21 of the Constitutions under the Right of Victim of crime provides:12

Right of Victim of Crime: (1) A victim of crime shall have the right to get information about the investigation and proceedings of a case in which he or she is the victim.

(2) A victim of crime shall have the right to justice including social rehabilitation and compensation in accordance with law.

The Constitution of Nepal for the first time in the world has engraved the rights of the Crime Victim. The victim of crime not only has the right to get information regarding the investigation and proceeding of the case but also the right to justice including social rehabilitation and compensation in accordance with law. This constitutional provision has a wider connotation. According to this Constitutional provision the crime victim has the right to get information about the proceeding of the case at every step of criminal process from investigation to prosecution, trial, appeal and execution of the judgement. The investigating and prosecuting authorities as well as the court have the duty to inform the victim of the proceeding of the case. The crime victim also has the right to justice including social rehabilitation and compensation.

PROVISION OF GENERAL LAW REGARDING
THE RIGHTS OF CRIME VICTIMS

There have been very few legal provisions regarding to the rights of crime victims before the commencement of this Act. These provisions have limited connotation and are limited only to some aspects of victim’s right like protection of the victim, privacy and compensation. These provisions do not cover the most important aspect of right that is the right to access to justice. However, the statutes drafted after the commencement of the constitution, i.e. the Muluki Criminal Code, 2017 and Muluki Criminal Procedure Code, 2017 and the Sentencing Act, 2017 have incorporated important provisions regarding the right of crime victims.

The Human Trafficking and Transportation (Control) Act, 2007 has made some provision about the rights of crime victims including the protection of the confidentiality of the crime victim and right to compensation. ‘Caste Based Discrimination and Untouchability (Offence and Punishment) Act, 2068 (2011)’ has criminalized the act of discrimination and made provision for restitution to the victim. Likewise, the Children Act, 2018 has ensured various rights of child victims including the right to information the language he or she understands, to participate, right confidentiality, right to compensation from the offender as well as from State, right to free legal aid and socio-psychological counselling service, right to free service of the translator, sign language expert or interpreter, right to get free of cost, the copy of the documents including the decisions and orders, right to get police protection for safety against the probable.13 Domestic Violence (Crime and Punishment) Act, 2066 (2009) offered for immediate protection of the victim and protecting her from further victimization,14 provision of in camera proceedings and hearings of the complaint.15 Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Act, 1993 has the provision of compensation and medical expense to be paid to the victim by the offender in case of loss of life, injury to body or loss of property.16

The codes relating to criminal justice including the Muluki Criminal Code, 2017, Muluki Criminal Procedure Code, 2017 and Sentencing Act, 2017 and rules thereof have been brought into commencement of the Constitution of Nepal in 2015. Section 32 of the Country Criminal Code guarantees the same right in the similar term under the title ‘Victim of crime to be entitled to obtain information of proceedings of case and compensation’. Section 13(c) of the Criminal Offence (Determination and Execution of Sentence) Act, 2017 has provided that the court, while determining the sentence has to take into account of delivering justice along with compensation to the victim.17

RIGHTS OF THE CRIME VICTIM UNDER CRIME
VICTIM PROTECTION ACT, 2018

Constitutional guarantee of the rights of crime victims and formulation of Crime Victim Protection Act, 2075 has been significant achievement in the field of rights of crime victims. The Act has classified victim as ‘Victim of First Grade’, ‘Victim of Second Grade’ and ‘Family Victim’ and made provision for providing compensation and relief. The rights guaranteed by the Act can be discussed under the following headings:

Right to Protection and Security

It is the foremost right of the victim to be protected from further victimization her/him and his/her family from the offender. Protection includes protection from attack, loss, fear, threat or intimidation that may be against the victim or the close relative of the victim and the person dependent on the victim from the suspected person, accused, convict or person related to the convict or the witness of accused.18

Right to Fair Treatment and Non-discrimination

Victim shall have the right to receive civilized, noble, fair and dignified treatment in criminal justice process.19 Victim shall not be discriminated in the criminal justice process on the basis of his or her religion, race, sex, caste, ethnicity, gender, origin, language, marital status, family status, age, physical or mental illness, disability or ideology or such other basis.20 However, the victims with the special needs like minor, elderly citizens or physically or mentally incapable persons have to be considered in the context of criminal justice process.

Right to Privacy

The Act has also guaranteed the victim of some sensitive crimes like rape, incest, human trafficking, sexual harassment, the right to privacy in the course of investigation, inquiry, prosecution and judicial proceedings.21 If any deed and testimony or deputation has to be made in the course of investigation, inquiry and judicial proceedings of the crimes it has to be prepared by presenting victim in a way to not reveal his or her identity. It has to be made by changing the real voice of the victim. If video conference has to be made in a way the offender is unable to see or hear.

Right to Information Relating to Investigation

The investigation officer, prosecutors and the court have to provide information regarding the investigation, prosecution and court proceeding in the case. It enables the victim to know the progress of the case as well as take further step in protecting her interest and securing justice.

Right to Express Opinion22

The victim’s right to express opinion about the process of making decision relating to prosecution, plea bargaining, mitigating the sentence, making additional charges, making pre-sentencing report, determining sentence and making decision regarding diversion, probation, parole, suspended sentence, open prison, community service or any other facilities of similar nature to the offender.23

Right to Participation

Another important right of the victim is the right to be present and make one’s statement in the proceedings relating to the court hearing in relation to the crime.24 The victim has the right to express opinion in the sentencing process also.

Right to Make Written Application or Appeal

The victim has the right to make a written application before the concerned agency or authority requesting for filing an application or appeal against the order of the court if Government of Nepal has the right to file an application or appeal against such order or decision of the court according to prevailing law.25

Right to Compensation

Right to receive compensation is another important right of the crime victim for realization of justice. Section 18 provides that if the victim is entitled to get compensation according to this Act or other prevailing law and if he/she requests for information about it the prosecuting authority should give information to the victim in relation to the procedures to be fulfilled relating to receiving compensation.26 Section 19 of the Act provides that the victim shall have the right to receive compensation for the loss he/she suffered.27

Right to Stay in a Separate Chamber During the Hearing

In the course of the hearing of the offence, the court may provide a separate chamber for the victim so that he or she can stay separately from the accused, person related to the accused and witness of the accused.28 If it is not possible to provide separate chamber make necessary arrangement for the safety and interest of the victim so that the accused, person related to the accused and witness of the accused cannot contact the victim, except as otherwise ordered by the court.

Right to Have Property Returned

The victim has the right to have his or her property taken under control during investigation or for evidence, returned immediately after the completion of investigation. However, if such property is to be submitted to the court for evidence or there is a dispute as to the ownership or possession of the property, the property shall not be returned before the dispute is settled. The court may, if it so thinks necessary, make an order to return such property before the dispute is settled.29

Right to Social Rehabilitation

Rehabilitation is a crucial right of the crime victim to enable him/ her live a normal life. The victim should be protected from further victimization and revictimization. It depends on the context, such measures range from external oversight and monitoring mechanisms to the establishment of judicial remedies, the strengthening of the judiciary’s independence. The Act has a provision regarding social rehabilitation of the victim however, it has not defined what social means.

Interim Compensation

According to the Section 29 of the Act in case victim has to be immediately treated or if compensation or any form of relief amount has to be provided, the court may give order to conduct medical treatment or to provide interim form of compensation or relief for such person. Such compensation shall be provided from the Fund.

Right to Appoint Legal Practitioner

Right to access to justice includes the right to effective representation which is not possible without legal practitioner. Although, the prosecutor represents the victim in the court, the victim also has the right to representation with the help of a law practitioner. The Act ensures that crime victims have the right to appoint a separate legal practitioner in the criminal justice process if they wish.30

ROLE OF SUPREME COURT IN THE PROTECTION
OF RIGHTS OF CRIME VICTIMS

The Supreme Court of Nepal has played an important role in the protection of the rights of victims of crime. It has taken a proactive approach in ensuring access to justice, safeguarding victims, and securing compensation and restitution for them. The Court’s decisions and orders reflect its commitment to these vital protections.

VICTIM’S RIGHT TO ACCESS TO JUSTICE

The Supreme Court of Nepal has issued a landmark ruling in the case crime victim Suntali Dhami31 reinforcing the protection of the right to access justice. The Court held that the malafide and arbitrary exercise of prosecutorial power is subject to judicial review. Likewise, Supreme Court in writ of mandamus of Punyashila Dawadi Ghimire vs. PMO32 and others held that:

The state, for purpose of making the access to justice easy has to adopt proper measures. The environment of realizing the care and protection of state by the victim has to be created. The state has to take measures to stop the barriers that discourage the women victim of rape and human trafficking from seeking judicial remedies and have been forced to live with mental, physical and psychological suffering.

The Supreme Court has made similar order in the writ of Certiorari and Mandamus of Jyoti Lamsal vs. GoN, PM and PMO33 and in the writ of certiorari of Narayan Singh Rajpoot vs. Office of the Attorney General of Nepal.34 In Narayan Singh Rajpoot case the Supreme Court held:

There is the provision of police for carrying out investigation of crime and the government has also made the provision of Government Attorney to screen the evidence, prosecute and defend the case in the court on behalf of the victim.

The Supreme Court, then the writ of Mandamus of Meera Dhungana vs. PMO35 issued a six-point directive to the respondents, instructing them to establish a plan to provide the victim with information about the investigation from the concerned police office. The directives also required the prosecutor to inform about the charges and court’s judgement, including copies of the charge sheet and judgement.

CONFIDENTIALITY OF THE VICTIM

Protection of the confidentiality of victim is directly connected with her physical protection. Supreme Court of Nepal has played very important role in the protection of confidentiality of victim. The Supreme Court, in a writ petition for Mandamus filed by Sapana Pradhan Malla, issued a directive order to the Government of Nepal, the Council of Ministers and the Ministry of Law and Justice to formulate laws for maintaining confidentiality in special types of cases involving women, children or individuals infected from HIV Aids, outlining the rights and responsibility of the concerned parties. The Court also issued directives consisting of 13 Sections to remain effective until such a law is formulated. In the order, the Supreme Court held that,36 in the human trafficking case of Sunil Lama vs. GoN, it analysed the principle of victimology for the first time and concluded that:

. . . the defendant alluring the victim of marrying her took to brothel of India and sold her. The victim got out of the life of hell of the brothel, lodged FIR and her statement has been certified from the court . . . the statement of the victim certified from the court is admissible as evidence even if she has not testified in the court.37

SUPREME COURT APPROACH REGARDING COMPENSATION

Compensation is essential for protecting and assisting victims of crime. The Supreme Court before the commencement of Crime Victim Protection Act, 2018 developed a procedure for compensating rape victims due to the absence of clear recovery procedure. In GoN vs. Swosti Baral, the Court held that it is the government’s duty to claim compensation for the victim by ascertaining the defendant’s property details and specifying the grounds and reasons for recovering the compensation in the charge sheet.38 The Supreme Court in the rape case of Rakesh Kumar Singh vs. GoN39 held that in rape cases the District Court has to give information to the victim after final judgement and execute the judgement as well as recover the compensation to the victim. This although, is not written in the law. The most significant and inspiring judgement regarding compensation to the victim is the ruling of the Court in Lok Bahadur Sharki et al. vs. GoN.40 The Supreme Court held that:

From now onwards, it is appropriate to provide compensation fixed by the final judgement of the court immediately to the victim of the crimes of such nature in which compensation is payable, by establishing a separate fund by the government and recover it from the offender as government due. Therefore, it is required to make necessary infrastructure by the government in this regard.

CONCLUSION

The new Constitution of Nepal has brought a paradigm shift in the role and status of victims in the criminal justice system. The Crime Victim Protection Act, 2018, has been enacted and implemented for translating these rights into practice. The Act has been implemented and the rights of crime victims have been safeguarded. A Victim Relief Fund has been established and a relief fee is levied from every convict in a criminal case. However, the situation of crime victim cannot change overnight, as it is not easy to implement new concepts in practice. The rights enshrined in the Constitution or laws must be put into practice through the collaborative efforts of all relevant stakeholders, which is a very challenging task. The government must manage this effectively. A clear understanding of the concept related to victims’ right and protection measures is essential for safeguarding victims’ right in every stage of the criminal justice procedure. To internationalize the concept of victim justice among key stakeholders, sensitization programmes should be carried out. Fundamentals of victim protection must be included in training course designed for judicial cadres, prosecutors, police investigators and defence lawyers. Additionally, a crime victim information mechanism should be established within police stations and public prosecutor offices to provide regular updates on the progress of their cases. Various legislations contain the provisions related to victim protection and compensation. Harmonization of these legal provisions is essential to ensure consistency in practice.
