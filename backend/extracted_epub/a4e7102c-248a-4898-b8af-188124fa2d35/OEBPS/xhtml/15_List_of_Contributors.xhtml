List of Contributors

MANOJ KUMAR JENA is Professor of Sociology and Chairperson at the Study of Social Systems, School of Social Sciences, Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi. He is the coordinator of the International Global Studies Programme of JNU, New Delhi. He has also coordinated an International Collaborative Teaching Programme with the Department of Sociology, York University, Canada. He also taught in the Department of Sociology, Jamia Millia Islamia, New Delhi. His areas of research interests are: Sociology of Science and Technology, Digital Technology Studies, Media Studies, and Ritual Studies. His writings hve appeared in many national and international peer-reviewed journals. His recent publications include Sociology of Covid 19 Pandemic in India: Education, Digital Divide, Work and Wellbeing (ed.) (2022) and Ways of Being Indian: Essays on Religion, Gender and Culture (ed.) (2024).

RENUKA SINGH is a former Professor of Sociology at the Centre for the Study of Social Systems, School of Social Sciences, Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi. She has been working in the area of Gender Studies, Diaspora, and Buddhist Studies for over four decades, and travels extensively delivering lectures at various seminars and universities. She has been associated with the Women’s Studies Centre, University of Delhi, Centre for Social Research, and was a Research Fellow at Centre for Cross-Cultural Research on Women at Oxford University. She is currently the Director of Tushita Mahayana Meditation Centre, New Delhi, and the Chairperson of Punjabi Sahitya Sabha, New Delhi. She has authored and edited several articles and books that have been translated into many languages of the world. Some of the titles are: The Womb of Mind, Women Reborn, The Path to Tranquility, The Transformed Mind, Many Ways to Nirvana, The Path of the Buddha, Becoming Buddha, Dalai Lama’s Little Book of Buddhism, Dual Identity: Indian Diaspora and Other Essays, Boundless as the Sky, Dalai Lama’s Little Book of Mysticism, The Little Book of Encouragement, and The Greatest Punjabi Stories Ever Told.

M.M. REHMAN is a former Senior Fellow at V.V. Giri National Labour Institute, Noida. He holds PhD degree in Economics. He worked at the National University of Educational Planning and Administration, (formerly, National Institute of Educational Planning and Administration). He extensively worked on issues such as informal sector workers, women workers, and social security of workers. He has conducted action research/research and number of evaluation studies on different fields related to education and labour. He also conducted a substantial number of training programmes for different groups comprising officials and activists. He has published several books and research monographs on topics related to education, jobholders in the informal sector, social security of workers, etc.

KANTA REHMAN holds a Ph.D in Political Science. She is the author of several books on human rights. She has worked in several organizations and institutions designated for research and education. She was initially an active community organizer and worked among the marginalized sections of the society. She has also done a lot of work in the field of action research for slum dwellers and has set up various informal schools.

KURUKHETRA DIP is an Assistant Professor of Sociology at the Department of Sociology, Faculty of Social Sciences, Banaras Hindu University. He previously taught at the Department of Sociology, Maa Manikeshwari University (formerly, Kalahandi University). His research areas include disability studies, cinema studies, sociology of health, and visual anthropology. He has completed his MA, M.Phil., and Ph.D. from Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi. Currently, he is serving as a board member (Board of Directors) of the Society for Disability Studies (SDS).

NESHAT QUAISER is an Associate Researcher with the Centre de Sciences Humaines (CSH), Delhi. He was the former Professor and Head of the Department of Sociology, Jamia Millia Islamia, Central University, Delhi. His research interests include: Critical Social Theory; Historical Sociology of Indian sub-continental medical traditions with a specific focus on Unani medicine; Muslims, Islam, Sharia, and Communalism in Indian sub-continent; Culture Religion and Society; Historical Sociology of Urdu language and literature; Colonialism and colonial politics; Post-Colonial Law and Legal Theory; Religion, Nationalism, and Peasant Politics in Indian subcontinent with the reference to Muslim politics and society; and inter-religious public debate during the colonial period.

RATHEESH KUMAR works as an Associate Professor at the Centre for the Study of Social Systems, School of Social Sciences, Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi, India. He has previously taught at the Department of Anthropology, University of Hyderabad and at the School of Interdisciplinary and Trans-disciplinary Studies, Indira Gandhi National Open University (IGNOU), New Delhi. He was a Charles Wallace India Trust Visiting Fellow at the Centre for Research in Arts, Social Sciences and Humanities (CRASSH), University of Cambridge (2021-2022). His recent publications include the coedited volume, Practising Interdisciplinarity: Convergences and Contestations (2024).

BISWAJIT DAS is the Professor and founding Director of Centre for Culture, Media and Governance. He has over four decades of teaching and research experience in the field of theory, method, and history of communication in India. He has published in various national and international journals. He is also the founding President of All India Communication and Media Association in India. His publications include Media and Mediation (2005), Social and the Symbolic (2007), Communication, Culture and Confrontation (2010), Gandhian Thought and Communication (ed.) (2020), and Caste, Communication and Power (ed.) (2021). His latest publications are: Seeing South Asia: Visuals beyond Borders (ed.) (2022), Korean Wave in South Asia: Transcultural flow, fandom and Identity (ed.) (2022) and Pedagogy in Practice: Project-Based Learning in Media policy & Governance(ed.), (2022).

RIDHI KAKKAR is a Research Scholar and Teaching Assistant at the Ashank Desai Centre for Public Policy, Indian Institute of Technology-Bombay. She has earned her M.A. and M.Phil. in Media Governance from the Centre for Culture, Media & Governance, Jamia Millia Islamia, New Delhi. She takes an interest in media and communication research. Currently, she is pursuing her PhD degree in the area of data governance and issues related to its monetization, access, and usage.

SHRUTI DEVGAN is an Assistant Professor of Sociology at Bowdoin College, USA. She holds a Ph.D. in Sociology from Rutgers University, USA. Her research interests include transnational flows, collective memory, trauma, narratives, media, Asian and American studies. She is currently working on a book on the digital, diasporic, and intergenerational memories of the anti-Sikh violence of 1984.

BINU SUNDAS is an Assistant Professor, Department of Sociology, Miranda House. Before joining Miranda House, he was teaching at Sikkim University. He is one of the founders of Sikkim University. He is also a Visiting Faculty at the Indian Institute of Management, Rohtak. He has authored two books, co-authored a book, and has many publications to his name. He has also presented many research papers at national and international conferences and seminars. He is an alumnus of Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi and Goethals Memorial School, Kurseong. He was also a football player of repute, and represented North Bengal University and he also played in the Calcutta League.

DIVYA PRADHAN is an Associate Professor, Department of English, Mata Sundri College for Women. She has many publications to her name and has presented many papers in international and national seminars and conferences. Her areas of interest are Indian Writing in English and Indian Philosophy. She is an alumnus of Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi.
