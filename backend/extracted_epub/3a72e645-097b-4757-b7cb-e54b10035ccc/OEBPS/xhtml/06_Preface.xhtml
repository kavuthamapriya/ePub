Preface

As the editor of Conflict, Peace and Human Security in South Asia, I am profoundly honoured to present this timely and significant collection of scholarly works. This edited volume emerges against a backdrop of rapid geopolitical shifts and enduring quests for peace and security within the South Asian region. The chapters herein offer a mosaic of perspectives, each delving into the intricate tapestry of South Asia’s political, legal and social landscapes.

The book opens with an incisive analysis of Nepal’s political evolution, tracing the rise of communist ideology from the peripheries to the centre stage of mainstream politics. It reflects on the historical and contemporary forces that have shaped the political parties and their ideologies culminating in the establishment of the Federal Republic of Nepal.

In a region where patriarchal norms have long dictated societal structures, the second chapter marks a significant departure by examining the post-conflict political participation of women in Nepal. It is a testament to the resilience and empowerment of women who have emerged from the shadows of conflict to claim their rightful place in the political arena.

The subsequent chapters navigate through the Nepali legal framework, advocating for the empowerment of crime victims and dissecting the foundational elements that contribute to the durability and stability of democratic constitutions. These sections emphasize the importance of robust legal and constitutional frameworks in fostering a just and equitable society.

‘Fragmented Unions’ offers a critical lens on the social facets of cohabitation within contemporary capitalist societies, while insights from Buddhist principles offer a unique perspective on security issues, reflecting the deep-seated cultural and religious influences that continue to shape South Asian societies.

The plight of Bhutanese asylum seekers in Nepal is poignantly captured in a chapter that explores the tryst with destiny faced by these individuals. The narrative resonates with the broader themes of displacement and statelessness that affect many across the region.

China’s expanding role in South Asia is meticulously examined, highlighting the economic, geopolitical and cultural dimensions of its influence. This chapter is particularly relevant in the context of China’s growing presence and the implications it holds for the regional power dynamics.

Further, into the intricate dynamics of Nepal-India relations within the broader context of China’s growing influence in South Asia this analysis examines Chinese activism in the region and its impact on the United States’ long-standing role as a global superpower. It delves into the US foreign and security policy initiatives that shape the peace and stability of Asia, while also considering implications for the US’s global superpower status.

Adopting a comprehensive approach, this research scrutinizes the implications of leadership changes in both the US and India, exploring how these shifts affect the power balance in contemporary Asia. It also evaluates policies of the new administration towards US allies, which are pivotal in shaping regional security and stability, heralding a new era in US foreign policy.

Moreover, the study investigates the effects of President Trump’s policy of bilateralism, focusing on its long-term positive and negative impacts on US foreign policy in Asia. Finally, it offers insights into the potential benefits of bilateralism for US interests in the economic and security domains.

Each chapter, while distinct in focus, collectively weaves a narrative that is both diverse and interconnected. The authors, through their rigorous research and insightful analyses, invite readers to engage with the complex realities of South Asia—a region that continues to grapple with the dualities of conflict and peace, tradition and modernity and security and vulnerability.

I hope that this book will serve as a valuable resource for scholars, policymakers and anyone interested in the multifaceted issues of conflict, peace and human security in South Asia. May it inspire continued dialogue and contribute to the pursuit of a more peaceful and secure future for all who call this region home.

PURNA BAHADUR KARKI
